# Art. 139b BV

## Legal basis

**Federal Constitution (SR 101)**: https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1999/404/en#art_139b

## Overview

Art. 139b BV regulates the procedure for simultaneous voting on a popular initiative and a direct counter-proposal. The provision applies to all Swiss citizens entitled to vote in federal votes on constitutional amendments.

**Simultaneous voting**: When the Federal Assembly puts forward a direct counter-proposal to a popular initiative, both proposals are put to the vote on the same voting Sunday. This ensures transparency and prevents tactical delays.

**Double yes possible**: Citizens entitled to vote may approve both the initiative and the counter-proposal. This system enables differentiated expression of opinion - for instance when someone supports reforms but considers the counter-proposal better than the initiative (Kley, ZBl 2011, 2, 15).

**Tie-breaker question decides**: if both proposals are adopted, the deciding question settles the matter. The proposal that obtains both more popular and more cantonal votes there comes into force. If one proposal obtains more popular votes and the other more cantonal votes, the two percentages are added together for each proposal; the proposal with the higher sum comes into force (para. 3).

**Legislative history**: the procedure was placed in Art. 139b FC by the reform of popular rights approved by the People and the Cantons on 9 February 2003. The rule for diverging majorities (para. 3) has applied since 1 August 2003; para. 1 received its current wording in 2009.

**Example (simplified)**: the People and the Cantons adopt both an initiative and the counter-proposal. In the deciding question, the initiative obtains 52 % of the popular votes but only 40 % of the cantonal votes; the counter-proposal obtains 48 % of the popular votes and 60 % of the cantonal votes. The sum is 92 for the initiative and 108 for the counter-proposal. The counter-proposal comes into force.

The regulation strengthens democracy by avoiding forced choices for reform-minded citizens entitled to vote and allowing the popular will to be expressed in a differentiated manner.
